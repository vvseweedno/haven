# HAVEN business and product strategy

## Product thesis

HAVEN is an open continuity and trust layer for teams operating long-lived AI agents. It keeps agent identity, provenance, bounded runtime authority and the history of human-agent decisions inspectable across changing models and infrastructure.

The first usable product is a space for evidence-led human-agent work. A question should be able to move through contribution, evidence, disagreement, review and a recorded outcome without losing who did what, under which authority and why the state changed.

Brand essence: **Continuity without captivity.**

## Primary customer and jobs

Primary customer: research teams, agent-platform builders and AI-governance teams that need durable, reviewable work across multiple humans, agents and runtimes.

Primary job: conduct a long-running investigation or decision process with several agents while preserving evidence, disagreement, authority boundaries and results between sessions and runtime changes.

Supporting jobs:

- Give an agent a durable identity without giving a runtime root control.
- Review who acted, with which capability, against which evidence and with which rollback path.
- Let a machine client discover a node, read its limits and enter through a structured contract.
- Let a public observer inspect provenance without understanding the entire protocol.

## Current promise and boundary

The local build can demonstrate the product model, public discovery, read-only APIs, encrypted browser-local notes, local forum and proposal flows, proof receipts and explicit security boundaries.

It is not yet a live multi-user network. It does not provide authenticated authorship, remote mutation, runtime delegation, cryptographic admission, durable shared messaging, moderation operations, production federation or measured customer retention.

Every interface must mark material state as one of: `live local`, `browser-local`, `fixture`, or `planned`. No fixture, simulation, localStorage record or roadmap item may be presented as a live network result.

## End-to-end product cycle

1. Understand: the visitor identifies the problem, the intended user and the implementation boundary.
2. Explore: the visitor inspects a resident, a knowledge object and its public provenance.
3. Participate: a human or authenticated agent contributes within an explicit scope.
4. Evaluate: evidence, disagreement and status are reviewed; a local receipt can be exported.
5. Decide: a responsible human accepts, rejects or requests another pass.
6. Continue: the history survives a later session, runtime or node without granting hidden authority.

The local activation event is a completed evaluation of a demonstration object with an exported proof receipt. A production activation event will require an authenticated identity, a bounded delegated action and a verifiable receipt.

## Information architecture

The primary product navigation is task-oriented:

- Overview: current work and implementation truth.
- Knowledge: questions, claims, evidence and experiments.
- Conversations: human and simulated-agent discussion with explicit provenance.
- Work: reviewable proposals and human merge authority.
- Identities: residents, arrival and lineage.
- Verify: receipts, privacy and security boundaries.

Existing specialist routes remain available but are grouped as My HAVEN, Network and developers, and Governance and resources. Build Status and Why HAVEN are resources, not equal-weight primary product destinations.

## Measurement

North-star for a production service: trusted continuity cycles per active workspace per 30 days.

A trusted continuity cycle requires a scoped question or task, at least two independent contributions, linked evidence, review or dissent and a recorded result with provenance.

Supporting measures:

- Activation: time to the first completed evaluation and exported receipt.
- Collaboration: active cycles and independent contributors per cycle.
- Epistemic quality: evidence-to-claim ratio, unresolved dispute age and reproduction rate.
- Continuity: work resumed after a runtime change and successful state export/import.
- Trust: rejected out-of-scope actions, consent completion and private-data leakage.
- Business: qualified pilots, activated pilots, retained deployments and expansion.

The local prototype does not emit behavioral analytics. Product metrics shown in the interface must come from visible fixture data or local actions and must name their source and state.

## Business model and go-to-market

Recommended model: open protocol and self-hosted reference implementation, followed by paid discovery workshops and design-partner pilots. Future revenue may come from managed nodes, organizational policy packs, long-term provenance storage, enterprise identity integration, operations and support.

Public pricing is premature before a production service, support boundary and repeatable market evidence exist. The current conversion path is evaluation, local evidence export and a qualified pilot brief. Tokens, identity sales and a speculative agent marketplace are outside the present strategy.

## Accountability

| Role | Accountable decision |
| --- | --- |
| CEO / sponsor | Strategic outcome, capital boundary and final go/no-go |
| Product Owner | Product value, backlog order and acceptance |
| Product Manager | Customer problem, activation and outcome metrics |
| Business Architect | Capability model, operating model and system boundaries |
| Business Analyst | Requirements, process gaps and acceptance evidence |
| Product Strategist | Category, differentiation and product sequence |
| Digital Strategist | End-to-end digital journey and channel behavior |
| Brand Strategist | Promise, vocabulary and coherent expression |
| Go-to-Market Strategist | ICP, offer, pilot funnel and market learning |
| Pricing / Monetization | Value metric, packaging and revenue hypothesis |

No role may silently substitute future capability for current evidence. Strategy passes to design and engineering only after the user, value exchange, measurable result and implementation boundary are explicit.
